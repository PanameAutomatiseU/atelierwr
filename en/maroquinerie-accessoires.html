Creative direction · Design · Development

Leather Goods & Accessories

Eleven projects that explore brand identity, volume, function and signature, from the first sketch to the development of the collection.

Each file starts from the codes of the house and uses to build a coherent creative direction. The research translates into silhouettes, clasps, details, materials, colors and families of products immediately recognizable.

The projects bring together bags, small leather goods, shoes, glasses, charms and cross-sectional research. The galleries present the essential steps of the process, from graphic studies to final proposals.

Eleven design approaches

01 Artistic direction project · Lemarys Paris

From brand mark
to object.

The Lemarys project combines volumes of classic and timeless bags with a strong artistic signature. Structured and elegant silhouettes become the support of rare know-how: leather work, application of gold leaf and stone leaf, and development of clasps inspired by the mineral world.

Thought as real sculptural elements, these clasps combine a precise geometry with a deliberately raw and precious material. Their presence transforms each bag into a piece of character, intended for a modern woman in search of an atypical accessory, high-end and immediately identifiable.

The process of overall artistic direction

For Lemarys Paris, I started by bringing together references to architecture, fashion, jewellery and materials. Moodboards gave a lead thread, the contrast between the crude and the sophisticated. I explored the sign of the brand in parallel with the volumes of bags. The tests of clasps, textures and finishes then connected the visual identity to the collection.

02 Creation & artistic direction · Mulberry

Equestrian
elegance.

Visual research and moodboarding

For this Mulberry project, I explored the idea of "Made in England" through references to the British heritage, the equestrian world and uniforms with Victorian ornament, dandysm and punk. By bringing these images together in my moodboards, I cleared up tracks of colours, materials and details, such as tartan, quilting, stripes and cords. These tracks guided my bags sketches, from the choice of volumes to the choice of finishes.

Creation and artistic direction of a collection of bags fed by Mulberry's British DNA. The volumes combine functionality, daily elegance and character, while the details draw from the world of horse riding: saddle lines, straps, flanges, braiding, looping evoking the walrus and finishes inspired by the saltwork.

Four proposals develop the same identity through complementary uses: compact saddle bag, soft cabas, structured pouch and small hand-carrying. Coloured leathers, metal fasteners and precise constructions make up a contemporary collection, immediately recognizable and faithful to a certain idea of British style.

03 Global artistic direction · Almé Paris

The Double A,
a collection signature.

Creation of the new Almé brand signature and its "double A" monogram, conceived as the starting point of a global universe. The sign is available in logotype, clasp, clasp, perforation, marking, pattern and detail finishing to install a strong identity, immediately recognizable and coherent across all collections.

The artistic direction accompanies the definition of accessories and the development of leather goods, belts and shoes collections. Each application adapts the monogram to the use and the material — gilded metal, embossed or perforated leather, natural rope, braiding and curling pieces — while affirming a free, warm and contemporary look.

04 Application · Design of leather goods · Céline

Essential
forms.

Project of proposals for volumes and bag design realized as part of an application for the position of designer leather goods at Céline. Three models develop a personal interpretation of what the house could be: an architectural and minimalist vision, built around strong silhouettes, clear lines and a mastered presence.

Each proposal explores a different formal principle—faceted, circled or curved—while seeking the same economy of means. Cuts, handles and closing systems extend the geometry of the volume in order to create coherent, functional and immediately identifiable bags, in accordance with aesthetics and brand values.

05 Identity & collection design · Maison Rinceau

Create
is not yielding.

Creation of a complete brand universe for Maison Rinneau: construction of an ornamental monogram, development of its graphic versions and transformation of the sign into clasp, bag jewel, pattern and collection signature. The same vocabulary thus links the visual identity with the volumes of leather goods and installs an immediate recognition.

This project is presented from the designer's point of view, in the context of a dispute with the client concerning the settlement of the work and the right to exploit the creation. According to the creator, the proposal was used as a new identity without the expected remuneration being paid or a transfer of the exploitation rights being formalized.

This experience illustrates a real difficulty of the profession: a creation does not become freely exploitable because it has been ordered, shown or handed over. The search time, the design and the value of the sign must be recognized, remunerated and framed. Defending this principle also means defending the work of all designers in the face of the appropriation of their ideas.

06 Collection creation · Place Vendôme Paris

Jewellery
becomes a bag.

Creation of a collection of signature bags for Place Vendôme, developed from the aesthetic codes of the brand. Originally expressed in its jewels, the square with cut strips refers to the architecture of the iconic Place Paris. Here, it leaves the scale of the jewel to structure volumes, flaps, bellows, inserts and finishing details.

The collection translates this vocabulary into a family of bags with strong aesthetics, impacting and immediately recognizable. Each silhouette reinterprets the geometry of the pattern while building an identity specific to the leather goods of the house — with, in watermark, a nose foot to the other great luxury brands of Place Vendôme.

07 Design project · Fendi

Fendi
Dandy.

Because every man needs to keep the child hiding in him, Fendi Dandy offers a vision of the masculine accessory that invites the great children that we are to preserve the taste of play, dream and imagination, through objects designed for their adult life.

A different approach to luxury accessories for men, in line with what Fendi is today: luxury, shift and provocation. Graphic codes, colours and functional details make up an expressive, personal and assumed collection.

08 Creation & collection design · Bally

Bally
Sport.

Creation of sports leather goods proposals based on Bally's legacy, his travel relationship and his graphic archives. The research combines functional lines, flexible volumes, precise constructions and leather details to build a coherent, contemporary and immediately identifiable collection.

The project brings together three complementary developments: a search for sneakers for the Bally Sport Spring-Summer 2006 collection, a range of bags inspired by the vintage codes of the house and a bag specially designed to transport and protect shoes, made in partnership with Vogue US and Anna Wintour.

09 Design project · Bezeling

From sketch
to wear.

Design of collections of sunglasses and high jewellery frames: definition of formal vocabulary, drawing of faces and branches, variations of materials and colours, presentation mockups and development accompaniment.

The four boards bring together an architecturally built acetate frame, a range search, studies of precious branches and a round gold set model presented in situation.

  • 01 · Collection frameAnalysis of positioning, uses, faces and brand codes to define a consistent range.
  • 02 · Search for formsExploring the proportions of face, bridges, strappings and eyebrow lines, from daily model to manifest piece.
  • 03 · Design & ergonomicsDevelopment of branches, hinges, supports and volumes to balance identity, comfort, weight and wear quality.
  • 04 · Materials & coloursAcetates, black or translucent, gold and silver metals, smoked glasses and combinations of finishes.
  • 05 · High jewelleryIntegration of paving, diamonds and coloured stones into the structure of the branches without losing their finesse.
  • 06 · DevelopmentMockups face, profile and three-quarters, coloured variants, technical preparation, exchanges with manufacturers and prototype fit.
10 Personal research · Bag charms · Responsible project

Crazy
Charms.

This personal research imagines a family of fun charms for bags and keyrings. These small colorful characters bring movement, humor and a pop touch to bags that are sometimes very classic or lacking real personality.

Each charm is thought of as an accessory in its own right: an expressive object that can be hung, moved or collected. Volumes, fringes and colour assemblies transform leather falls from leather goods into desirable and singular products.

The approach combines creative freedom and re-use of the material. It makes it possible to enhance small pieces of leather that are difficult to use for a bag, while offering a light, playful and responsible collection.

  • 01 · Fun & popGraphic characters able to wake up a sober bag and customize the wearer.
  • 02 · Dual useAccessories designed to function as bag charms or keychains.
  • 03 · ConstructionOverlays, fringes, seams and small pieces assembled to give volume and movement.
  • 04 · Re-useA creative use of leather falls from leather goods workshops and trades.
11 Transversal project · Accessories

Sketches &
research.

This selection brings together studies and projects of different natures. It shows how an idea is built up gradually, from the first gesture drawn up to the definition of a volume, use or collection.

The drawing allows me to explore several directions freely before specifying a proposal. Each project is presented separately in order to make its intention, its function and the choices that structure its development legible.

01 · Creative approach
Drawing as
starting point.

My creative process always goes through a phase of research through hand-held sketches. I quickly draw up silhouettes, compare proportions, test port systems and understand the construction of an object before detailing it.

This board brings together the first intentions. The line remains free and spontaneous: it serves to multiply the hypotheses, to eliminate certain leads and to make emerge a coherent direction.

02 · Shoes · Search for silhouettes
Slippers of footwear

This board explores shoe lines, their proportions and the relationship between the stem, heel and sole. The sketch allows you to quickly compare several attitudes before specifying a direction.

03 · Technical clothing · Travel
Jingo

This project studies a technical garment designed for travel. Research organizes uses, functional details and different parts in order to build a coherent range.

04 · Personal research · Fashion
Burberry

This personal research combines fashion silhouettes, bags and accessories. The board links the different elements of a collection and explores how they can share a formal direction.

05 · Baggage shop · Mobility
Nomadic luggage

Study of a storage system for the transport of a computer and a camera. The project works on interior organisation, equipment protection and different modes of carrying.

06 · Personal research · Leather goods
Sonia Rykiel

This personal study explores leather goods volumes and decorations. The proposals compare several bag silhouettes and how a graphic signature can fit into the product.

07 · Foldable baggage · Functional study
Kipling 2GO

Functional study of a foldable trolley. The drawing breaks down the kinematics, storage, handle and castors in order to make the principle of use immediately understandable.

08 · Women's Collection · Development
Kipling · Thrill Collection

Development of a female collection conceived as a family of bags. The research articulates silhouettes, uses, colours and volume development in order to maintain overall consistency.
